Section 3 cheat sheet: How much is done

About 1 minute · Functionality, 40%. Italics = say it. Blue = what's on screen.

Steps

  1. Open the presenter page and press 3 (Coverage). The bar: 15 complete (green), 7 partly built (amber), 19 next (grey).
  2. "The SRS has 41 Release 1 requirements. 15 are complete, which is 37%, and 7 more are partly built. The target was about 30%."
  3. Point at the module boxes. "Six of the nine back-end modules are built. The other three are empty packages for the next increments."
  4. Click Complete in the filter to show only finished requirements.
  5. Click Partly built and explain one example (below).
  6. Click All 41 to reset.

The key numbers

  • 41 Release 1 requirements in the submitted SRS (plus 6 Stretch ones, not counted).
  • 15 complete (37%), 7 partly built, 19 not started.
  • 6 of 9 modules built: members, inventory, settings, ledger, allocation, requests.
  • About 11,500 lines of app code: 7,712 Java and 3,809 TypeScript.

What is complete

  • Inventory: define Resource Types as data, then add, change and retire Resources.
  • Drafts: create a Request, add lines, mark them Required or Optional, and see live Quotes.
  • Submit: Quotes lock, and the Request is refused with the shortfall if the team can't afford it.
  • Rooms (Exclusive): no double-booking, plus the Waitlist.
  • GPUs (Metered): an hour-by-hour check against the pool size and the team Quota.
  • Credits: Accounts, the append-only transaction history, and the price formula.

What "partly built" means

The core is in and tested, but one piece is missing. Examples to quote:

  • Holds: holding and releasing a booking work in both allocators, but nothing places a Hold until the Pending state arrives with people-lending.
  • Grants: monthly Grants, mid-month Grants and Statements work. The job that rolls over to the next month is next.
  • Refunds: the refund formula is built and tested. The screen to release a booking is next.
  • Settings: prices and Quotas are stored as data and served by the API. The edit screens are next.

What is not built yet

  • Lending people (the Matched class): profiles, shortlists, approvals, Loans.
  • Priority bumping, releasing a booking, suggestions, the dashboard, adding members.

Why we built in this order

  • "Our design builds modules from the bottom up. Inventory, Settings and the Ledger don't depend on anything else, so they came first. Allocation and Requests sit on top of them."
  • "That gave us one complete path, from Inventory to a charge on the Statement, instead of many half-finished features."

If the grader asks

  • "How did you count 30%?" By requirements in the SRS: 15 of 41 are fully done. We count partial ones separately, not as done.
  • "Why no people-lending yet?" It depends on everything else: Holds, the Pending state, approvals, and earning credits. It's the whole of the next increment.
  • "Is this a mockup?" No. Every screen calls the real API, and the data is in PostgreSQL. The live demo shows it.
